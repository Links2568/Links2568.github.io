The guqin, a seven-string zither on UNESCO's list of Intangible Cultural Heritage, was the
instrument of China's literati from the Song through the Qing dynasties. Between 30,000 and
100,000 historical pieces survive in **jianzipu**, a shorthand tablature. Each character is built
from components that say where the hand goes and how the string is plucked, but none of them say
how long a note lasts or how a phrase moves: that was passed on from teacher to student.

**Strokes of Sonic Tradition** builds AI that can read this corpus and connect it to living
practice. It develops data-efficient optical music recognition shaped around the way jianzipu
characters are composed (Figure 1), an annotation studio that detects the strokes of each
character, and music-information-retrieval and computer-vision tools that align historical scores
with modern audio and video of guqin players.

The two-year project received $343K from Schmidt Sciences' Humanities and AI Virtual Institute
(HAVI), one of 19 teams in the program. It is led by Prof. Hao-Wen (Herman) Dong with David Sears,
Nathan Martin and Joseph Lam at the University of Michigan, Eduardo Veas at Graz University of
Technology and Ming Tak Ted Hui at the University of Oxford; the team also includes Ming Wilson,
Tristan Repolusk, Yuxuan Xin, Yikang Hong and me. A paper is in preparation.
